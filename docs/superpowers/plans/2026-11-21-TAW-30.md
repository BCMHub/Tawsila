Its sections 3 (data model), 4 (time and states), 5 (function contracts and error codes), 8 (lock order, scenarios 1–31, the job, expiry and scheduler evidence), 13 (test list, mutation and regression evidence) and 14 (expected-privilege rows and per-function outcomes) are **binding test sources**: every bullet there becomes at least one assertion, scenario or Jest case.

**Criteria:** TAW-30 scope revision 4, AC1–AC10, snapshot sha256 `9f4feee4031dbfd9ac5ec479c37199ea5fe7b50cffe7ddc9de54ac410f4a54ac`.

**Script lifecycle (P-F5).** `concurrency.sh` has two modes. Standalone (`concurrency.sh` or `concurrency.sh --scenario <n>`) resets the database first and installs a reset trap on every exit path. Prepared (`concurrency.sh --scenario <n> --no-reset`) runs one scenario against the database as the caller left it and never resets; it exits 0 on PASS, 1 on a scenario failure and prints the failing assertion id. Scenarios that need the pause (31 and the F107 case of 14) install the advisory-lock body of `taw30_test_pause()` at their start and restore the empty body with `create or replace` at their end (trap), so they never reset to restore it. `concurrency-mutations.sh` owns the resets: per mutation it resets, runs the baseline scenario in prepared mode (must PASS), applies the committed mutation, runs the same scenario in prepared mode (must fail with the named symptom, for the F107 mutation `too_many_pending`), then resets. `cron-run.sh`, `upgrade.sh` and `runbook-snippet.sh` commit to the local database and end with `supabase db reset` (trap on every exit path).
