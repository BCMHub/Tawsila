## 1. Purpose, decisions and boundaries

After Google sign-in a new user lands in `needs_profile` (TAW-7). TAW-4 turns that placeholder into onboarding (name, 18+, Terms and Privacy, community, role) and ends with a server-confirmed move to `ready_to_browse`. Separately, before the user's first ride, TAW-4 collects identity documents for manual review: National ID for everyone; driver's license, car license and car data for car owners. TAW-4 stores the data and the review status and exposes eligibility functions; it does not block browsing and does not build the ride features.

**Owner decisions** (card scope revision 6, design card):
1. Profile completion needs no identity documents; documents are required before the first ride, not before browsing.
2. Names are typed by the user; the Google name and email are never copied into a profile (TAW-7 AC11). The signed-in email may be shown as a read-only line from the session.
3. Community, car brand, model and type are dropdowns fed by configurable database lists (brand and model with a free-text Other, type with an explicit other entry); the lists are seeded once from an open dataset after its licence is checked and extended for the Egyptian market (section 7); no live third-party call at run time.
4. National ID number (14 digits) and photos (front and back) are mandatory for everyone. For car owners (role "I have a car" or "Both") the driver's license (photos, front and back, and expiry date) and the car license (photos, front and back, and expiry date) plus car brand, model, type, year and plate number are mandatory. A rider who later becomes a car owner must submit them before posting rides.
5. Plates mix Arabic letters and digits; digits-only is allowed.
6. Each document has a server-owned status (pending, approved, rejected) with a reviewer note; review is manual, by the owner, through the Supabase dashboard; automation later through the same states.
7. Expired documents, a grace period and renewal are a future feature; TAW-4 stores the expiry dates only.
8. The Egypt data-protection check is deferred by the owner and is a condition before real users' documents are collected (section 9).

**Out of scope:** phone number and OTP (TAW-5); the ride-action gate itself (section 8 defines the interface); logout and account deletion (separate PROPOSED cards, they must also delete stored documents); an admin review app; expired-document handling; automated validation; a license number or the name on the licenses (not collected, data minimization); production deployment; Android sign-in (section 12, Blocker B1).

## 2. Flows

**A. Onboarding (state `needs_profile`).** P1 profile (first name, last name; read-only signed-in email line) → P2 age and consent → P3 community dropdown (closed, open) → P4 role. One server call completes the profile; the app then re-derives state and moves to `ready_to_browse`. Frames: P1 199:1156, P2 186:670, P3 186:740, P3b 194:1092, P4 198:1104, plus dark variants.

**B. Verification (state `ready_to_browse`, before the first ride).** V1 intro → V2 and V3 National ID (number, front and back photos; invalid state) → for car owners only: V4 driver's license (photos, expiry date), V5a car details and car license expiry, V5b car license photos → V8 sending, V9 failed → V10 submitted (V10b for riders) → V11 approved or V12 rejected with reviewer note and resubmit. V6 is the photo source sheet, V7 the photo check. V1b is the intro for a rider who becomes a car owner. Entry points: a "Verify your identity" item in the Profile tab, a banner after onboarding, and the ride-action gate of later cards (section 8).

**C. Role change.** `set_role` changes the role at any time. Rider to car owner: the car-owner items become required and `can_post_rides` stays false until they are approved. Car owner to rider: stored data is kept; the requirement drops.

**Draft behaviour (Recommendation).** The verification flow keeps its input in memory and sends everything in V8; leaving the flow discards unsent photos. No document data is written to local storage.

## 3. Data model and authorization

All new tables live in `public`, have RLS enabled, and grant clients no insert, update or delete privilege. Writes happen only through `security definer` functions with an empty `search_path` (Recommendation, section 12 O8: SQL functions, not Edge Functions; this matches TAW-7 section 3 and needs no new infrastructure). Every function derives the user from `auth.uid()`, rejects a missing `accounts` row, is idempotent where stated, raises generic errors that never echo submitted values, and has execute revoked from `public` and `anon`.

**Configuration tables** (select for `authenticated`, no client writes; managed by the owner through migrations or the dashboard): `communities` (id, name, sort_order, active); `legal_documents` (kind terms or privacy, version, effective_at, is_current; one current row per kind by a partial unique index); `car_brands` (id, name, active); `car_models` (id, brand_id, name, active); `car_types` (id, code, name, active). The repository contains no pilot list: tests use clearly marked fixtures; real lists are loaded by the owner (section 7).

**User tables** (select own row only unless stated):

| Table | Key columns | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| `profiles` | user_id (pk, references accounts), first_name, last_name, community_id, role (`rider`, `car_owner`, `both`), timestamps | names validated by a pure SQL function; community must be active |
| `consents` | id, user_id, kind (`age_18`, `terms`, `privacy`), version (null for age_18), accepted_at | append-only; unique per (user, kind, version); versions must equal the current `legal_documents` row |
| `identity_records` | user_id (pk), national_id (text, `^[0-9]{14}$`) | no client select: the column is never returned to clients; reads go through `my_verification()` which returns only the last four digits |
| `verification_items` | (user_id, kind) pk with kind `national_id`, `driver_license`, `car_license`; status (`pending`, `approved`, `rejected`), revision (integer, starts at 1, +1 on every accepted submission), submission_key (uuid of the accepted submission), reviewer_note, reviewed_revision, expiry_date (licenses only), submitted_at, reviewed_at | a status is set to `pending` only by the `submit_*` functions and to `approved` or `rejected` only by `review_item` (section 3, "Review"); a trigger rejects every other change of status, note or reviewed_revision, including by the service role |
| `verification_photos` | (user_id, kind, side, revision) pk with side `front` or `back`; storage_path (unique), uploaded_at | a row is created only after the trusted object check (section 4); the photos of a revision are immutable; the item points to its current revision, so a replacement is a new revision, never an overwrite |
| `vehicles` | user_id (pk, one car per user, Recommendation O5), brand_id or brand_other (exactly one), model_id or model_other (exactly one), type_id (required), year, plate_number | plate stored normalized (section 6); year range from section 6; `car_types` contains an explicit `other` row, so the type needs no free text; **brand and model relationship:** a catalogue `model_id` is allowed only together with a catalogue `brand_id` and must belong to that brand (a composite key from the vehicle to the model's brand enforces it); with `brand_other` the model must be `model_other` (no catalogue model); with a catalogue brand the model may still be `model_other` for a model the list lacks; free-text values are trimmed, 1 to 50 characters; `submit_car` also checks that the selected brand, model and type are `active` |
| `verification_submissions` | (user_id, kind, submission_key) pk; revision, created_at; unique (user_id, kind, revision) | immutable history of accepted submissions (section 3, "Submission semantics"); written only by the `submit_*` functions |

**Functions (the contract):**
- `complete_profile(first_name, last_name, community_id, role, age_confirmed, terms_version, privacy_version)`: validates, writes `profiles` and the three consent rows, and sets `accounts.profile_completed_at` if null, in one transaction; idempotent for the same inputs; the only writer of that column (as TAW-7 requires). Documents are not checked. **Consent drift:** if either version is no longer current it raises `consent_version_stale` and writes nothing. The app then reads the current versions (select on `legal_documents`), shows P2 again with the new versions and requires a fresh explicit acceptance; it never resubmits with substituted versions silently. A test covers a version change between P2 and completion.
- `set_role(role)`.
- `submit_national_id(submission_key, national_id, front_path, back_path)`, `submit_driver_license(submission_key, expiry, front_path, back_path)`, `submit_car(submission_key, brand_id, brand_other, model_id, model_other, type_id, year, plate, license_expiry, front_path, back_path)`: each validates inputs (section 6), verifies both photos through the trusted check of section 4 (bucket, caller folder, kind and side, existence, size and type; a path not matching the convention is rejected), then in one transaction stores the rows, increments the item's revision, records the submission key and sets the item `pending`. `submit_driver_license` and `submit_car` require the caller's role to be `car_owner` or `both`.
- **Submission semantics.** The client creates one `submission_key` (a uuid) per kind and per attempt and reuses it for every retry of that attempt. Every accepted submission is recorded forever in an immutable history table `verification_submissions` (user_id, kind, submission_key, revision, created_at; primary key (user_id, kind, submission_key), unique (user_id, kind, revision)). **A key found in the history is a replay, whatever the item's current status or revision:** the call returns that submission's recorded result (its revision and a `superseded` flag if a newer revision exists) and changes nothing; it never inserts photo rows, never creates a revision and never touches the current status or any review decision. So A, then B, then a delayed retry of A leaves B current and unchanged, and a retry of A after approval of A or B changes nothing (safe against a lost response, a double tap or two devices). A key not in the history while the item is `none`, `pending` or `rejected` is a new submission: accepted as a new revision (`pending`; previous photos stay as history). A key not in the history while `approved`: rejected with `already_approved` (renewal belongs to the future expiry feature). The three kinds are independent: a failure of one does not undo or block the others, and V8/V9 show progress and retry per kind. Tests: duplicate key no-op, new key creates a revision, approved is protected, a retry after partial success keeps the successful kinds, concurrent submissions of the same item serialize (row lock) and end in one consistent revision.
- **Review.** The owner reviews through `review_item(user_id, kind, revision, status, note)`, executable by the service role only. It raises `stale_revision` unless `revision` equals the item's current revision, so a decision taken on submission A can never approve submission B; it sets `reviewed_revision`, `reviewed_at`, the status (`approved` or `rejected`) and the note. The reviewer sees the revision in the item and in the photo rows. Tests: stale revision rejected, a submission replaced during review keeps its new `pending` status, direct status updates rejected by the trigger, client calls impossible.
- `my_verification()`: returns, for the caller, each kind's status, revision, reviewer note, expiry date, whether both photos exist, and the last four digits of the National ID.
- `can_request_rides()` and `can_post_rides()`: the eligibility interface (section 8).

**Reviewer procedure.** The owner approves or rejects by calling `review_item` as the service role in the Supabase dashboard (SQL editor) and views the photos in the dashboard Storage browser; the exact statements are documented in `supabase/README.md`. Whether the dashboard can view private objects is **UNVERIFIED** (V3) and is proved on the test project before the plan is finalized.

## 4. Storage

One private bucket, `identity-documents`, created through `supabase/config.toml` (local) and the same settings on the hosted test project. Limits (Recommendation): `file_size_limit` 5 MB, `allowed_mime_types` `image/jpeg` only; the client re-encodes every photo to JPEG at a bounded size (section 5), which also drops EXIF metadata (to be verified, V6). Fact: the global limit on the free tier is 50 MB and a bucket limit may only be lower; buckets are private by default; uploads are blocked until policies exist; the service key bypasses RLS (sources in discovery).

**Path:** `<user_id>/<kind>/<side>-<uuid>.jpg`; a new object per upload, never an overwrite.

**Policies on `storage.objects` for this bucket only:** authenticated users may INSERT objects whose first folder equals their `auth.uid()` text and whose second folder is one of the three kinds; there is no SELECT, UPDATE or DELETE policy for clients (the app never reads photos back; resubmission uploads a new object). The `(storage.foldername(name))[1] = auth.uid()::text` form is **UNVERIFIED** against official text and is proved by a local test (V1). The MIME check may rely on the client-declared type: treat it as untrusted and verify with a test (V5).

**Integrity (mandatory trusted check).** Postgres cannot make a row require a stored file (Fact), and deleting `storage.objects` rows in SQL leaves the bytes behind (Fact). Therefore the app uploads first, then calls a `submit_*` function, and the function performs the **trusted check** before any photo row or eligibility can exist: the path matches `<caller user_id>/<kind>/<side>-<uuid>.jpg` for exactly the kind and side being submitted, the object exists in bucket `identity-documents`, its size is within the limit and its recorded type is `image/jpeg`. A submission that fails the check is rejected and nothing is stored. **There is no fallback to client-side or reviewer-only enforcement:** the eligibility functions (section 8) count an item only when its current revision has both verified photo rows. If the plan cannot prove such a check in a `security definer` function (V4), the card stops and reports to the owner before any other mechanism is chosen (for example an upload through a trusted function), and a weaker control is never substituted. Replaced and abandoned objects are orphans; removal uses the Storage API by the owner (service role) and is a documented manual step until a cleanup card exists (Recommendation, O6).

**Signed access.** Clients never receive public URLs. If a later card needs to display a document, it uses short-lived signed URLs (minutes); TAW-4 itself does not display photos back to the user except the local preview before upload.

## 5. App structure

- **Routing.** `(onboarding)` keeps its guard (not `ready_to_browse`); the `needs_profile` branch of the screen that today shows "Almost there" renders the onboarding wizard instead. A new route group `(verify)` is protected by `ready_to_browse`. The TAW-7 derivation, session invariants, storage adapter, install guard and exchange logic are not changed (Fact: they are the contract, discovery F22).
- **State refresh.** After `complete_profile` succeeds the app must re-derive state. Today only `retry()` does (Fact, discovery F21). TAW-4 adds one public `refresh()` on the auth context that runs the existing derive path; it adds no new derivation. The TAW-7 test suites (unit and real-SDK) must stay green and a regression test covers `refresh()`.
- **Data access.** The auth client type is narrowed to one `accounts` read (Fact, F20). TAW-4 adds a separate typed feature client for the RPCs, tables and storage, built on the existing singleton; it does not widen the auth client.
- **Screens and components** use only theme tokens and `strings.ts` (English only, as TAW-3 requires), matching the Figma frames. New shared components: text field, dropdown (a modal list built from React Native primitives; no new library; announced as a pop-up button), checkbox row, photo slot, status badge. No form library.
- **New native dependencies:** `expo-image-picker` and `expo-image-manipulator` (compatibility with SDK 58 per the Expo docs; to be confirmed by install, V7). They change native code, so a dev-client rebuild is needed; `app.json` gains camera and photo permission strings (English copy: why the photo is needed). Uploads read the file with the SDK 58 `File` API (`arrayBuffer()`) and use `supabase.storage.from(bucket).upload` with `contentType` `image/jpeg` and no upsert; the 2023 React Native zero-byte pitfall is checked on a device (V8).
- **Photo handling.** Camera or library via the picker with the system permission prompts; a denied or limited permission shows a message with a path to Settings; the image is resized (longest side bounded) and re-encoded to JPEG (quality bounded) by the manipulator before upload; no document scanner (Expo has none; third-party scanners are out of scope).
- **Temporary files.** The picker and the manipulator write photo files to the app's cache. The flow keeps them in one known cache folder and deletes them after a successful upload of that photo, on replacement ("Retake"), when the flow is abandoned or fails permanently, and on the next app start for any leftovers; whether the OS clears them itself is **UNVERIFIED** (V13) and is not relied on. Tests assert the cleanup calls.
- **Never logged.** The National ID, plate, photo paths and picker URIs are never written to console, analytics or error messages; a static test scans for it (section 10).

## 6. Validation rules (client and server, same rules)

- **Names:** trimmed; 1 to 50 characters; letters (Latin or Arabic), spaces, hyphen and apostrophe; no digits (Recommendation).
- **National ID:** exactly 14 digits; Arabic-Indic digits are normalized to ASCII first; format only. No check digit and no birth-date or governorate check (Fact: no official source for a check digit; a plausibility check would risk false rejections, V9). Using the encoded birth date for the 18+ check is a possible later card.
- **Plate:** normalized in SQL by NFKC, removing spaces, hyphens and tatweel, mapping Arabic-Indic digits to ASCII; valid when it is digits only (1 to 7), or a letters-and-digits mix with 1 to 3 letters from an explicit allowed letter list and 1 to 4 digits in either order (Recommendation). The exact letter list and lengths are **UNVERIFIED** (V10) and are confirmed by the owner. Regexes use explicit code points, never `\d`, `[[:alpha:]]` or ranges, because Postgres classes and ranges depend on collation (Fact; the same reason TAW-7 used an ASCII lookahead).
- **Year:** from 1980 to the current year plus one (Recommendation).
- **Expiry dates:** a valid calendar date within a sanity range (not before 2000-01-01, not more than 30 years ahead). A date in the past is stored, not rejected: AC13 defers all expired-document handling, and the reviewer decides on an expired license.
- **Photos:** both sides required for each kind; JPEG; at most 5 MB.
- **Car selection (client and server):** the model dropdown lists only the models of the chosen brand and is disabled until a brand is chosen; choosing brand "Other" switches both brand and model to free text; changing the brand clears the model; the server enforces the same relationship (section 3, `vehicles`) and rejects any other combination.
- **Consent:** age_18 requires an explicit true; terms and privacy versions must equal the current versions from `legal_documents`.

## 7. Configurable lists and seeding

Communities, legal versions, car brands, models and types are rows, not code, and no list is hardcoded in application code. Facts: NHTSA vPIC covers US-registered vehicles and is rate-limited, so it is never called at run time; the `us-car-models-data` repository carries a CC BY 4.0 licence file (attribution is required; third parties describe it differently); no open dataset covering Egypt-market brands was found.

**Seed deliverable (AC18, a TAW-4 task, not left to the owner).** Once, in the implementation, TAW-4 produces a committed car catalogue seed (brands, models, types, with the `other` type row) under `supabase/seed/` plus a loader and a short procedure. Steps, in this order, each recorded as evidence: (1) the owner picks the open dataset (Recommendation: the CC BY 4.0 `us-car-models-data` data) and its licence text is read from the source repository at the pinned commit; the card stops and reports if the licence is not usable; (2) a `NOTICE` file carries the required attribution; (3) the catalogue is built from the dataset by a documented script and a coverage report lists which Egypt-market brands and models are missing; (4) the missing Egypt-market brands and models are added in a separate, clearly labelled extension file that the owner reviews; (5) the seed loads on the local stack through the loader (loading it on the hosted test project belongs to the later hosted setup, section 15.1, and is not part of this implementation). Communities and legal versions ship as fixtures only (real communities and the real Terms and Privacy versions are owner content, loaded through the same procedure). "Other" for brand and model is stored in `brand_other` and `model_other` (free text, trimmed, bounded length); the type uses the explicit `other` row.

## 8. Eligibility interface (for TAW-5 and the ride cards)

`can_post_rides()` is true when the caller's role is `car_owner` or `both` and the National ID, driver's license and car license items are all `approved` for their current revision. `can_request_rides()` is true when the National ID item meets the configured level (Recommendation O2: `pending` or `approved` in the MVP so riders are not blocked on manual review, switchable to `approved` through one configuration row without an app update). In both functions an item counts only if its current revision has both photo rows created by the trusted check (section 4), so a `pending` item never grants anything without verified photos, and an `approved` status of an older revision never carries over to a new submission. Both are `stable`, `security definer`, return false for a missing row, and never raise. TAW-4 builds and tests the functions; the ride features call them and enforce them server-side (TAW-5 AC6 already requires server-side authorization); TAW-5 and the ride cards do not change in TAW-4 (discovery: TAW-5's criteria cover phone verification only; the spec does not assume they change).

## 9. Privacy, security and abuse

- National ID number and photos are the most sensitive data in the project. Only the owner of a row and the service role can read them; the number is never returned to clients (section 3); photos are never public; no table of TAW-4 is added to a realtime publication.
- Errors, logs and analytics never contain submitted values. Server functions raise generic codes.
- Consent sentence (AC14) is shown with the Terms and Privacy step (P2) and again on V1: why the ID and licenses are collected and who can see them.
- **Egypt gate (AC14, TAW-2).** Supabase has no Egypt region and Storage content may be served through a global CDN (Fact, TAW-2 ADR). The owner deferred the legal check. Condition recorded in this spec: until Law 151/2020 is assessed, only test identities and a test project are used; real users' documents are not collected; no production project is created.
- **Product document.** `docs/product/mvp.md` currently defers government ID and says vehicle fields are make, model, type, colour and seat capacity (discovery). TAW-4 changes this by owner decision; the amendment of `mvp.md` is a separate documentation change that needs the owner's approval (as in PR #5) and is not made by this card.
- Abuse: bucket size and type limits; submissions are idempotent by key and serialized per item (section 3); a per-day resubmission limit and orphan cleanup are listed for the owner (O6).
- Auth hardening is unchanged (TAW-7). `user_metadata` is never used for authorization.

## 12. Owner decisions, open items and blockers

- **O1 Profile photo.** The approved design (P1) shows an optional photo and `docs/product/mvp.md` lists a profile photo, but no TAW-4 criterion covers it. Recommendation: not in TAW-4 (it needs a different bucket with broader read access and has other privacy rules); P1 shows the initials circle without the camera control, a recorded deviation from the approved frame. Owner decides.
- **O2 Request gate level.** Recommendation in section 8 (pending or approved for requesting, approved for posting).
- **O3 Car list source.** Recommendation in section 7: the CC BY 4.0 `us-car-models-data` catalogue (attribution in a `NOTICE`) plus an owner-reviewed Egypt-market extension file; the owner confirms the dataset before the seed task starts.
- **O4 National ID storage.** Recommendation: plain text in a restricted table for the MVP (never selectable by clients), with the option to move to encrypted storage (Supabase Vault or pgcrypto) before real users; the format check needs no secret.
- **O5 One car per user** in the MVP (a second car needs a later card).
- **O6 Retention, cleanup and abuse.** Recommendation: keep documents while the account exists; orphan removal and deletion on account deletion are owned by the deletion card; a per-day resubmission limit.
- **O7 `mvp.md` amendment** (section 9) as a separate approved change.
- **O8 Function form.** SQL `security definer` functions (Recommendation) versus Edge Functions (no infrastructure exists).
- **O9 Entry points** of verification: Profile tab item, post-onboarding banner, ride-action gate (Recommendation; the banner copy and placement follow the design notes).

**15.1 Deferred (not built):** the tables `identity_records` and `verification_photos`; the functions `submit_national_id` and `submit_driver_license` and the photo paths and license expiry of `submit_car`; the storage bucket, its policies and the trusted object check; the capture, re-encode, upload and temporary-file service and its permission strings; the native dependencies `expo-image-picker` and `expo-image-manipulator` and the dev-client rebuild they need; the screens V1, V2, V3, V4, V5b, V6, V7, V8, V9 and V10b; the National ID, photo and expiry validators; the hosted storage setup and the hosted loading of the catalogue seed (the seed loads on the local stack only). **15.2 Contract delivered:**
1. **Manual approval only for people who post rides.** `verification_items` accepts only the kind `car_owner` in this implementation (a check constraint; the later card relaxes it by a new migration that adds the document kinds). A user who has never submitted car details has no verification item and needs no approval. A former car owner who switched back to the role `rider` keeps the stored vehicle and item (item 6); a retained item imposes no approval requirement on a rider: `can_request_rides()` ignores it and `can_post_rides()` is false for the role `rider` whatever the item says.
2. **`submit_car(submission_key, brand_id, brand_other, model_id, model_other, type_id, year, plate)`**: the caller's role must be `car_owner` or `both`; it validates the plate, year, the brand and model relationship and active entries (section 6), then in one transaction writes `vehicles`, appends the submission-history row, increments the `car_owner` item's revision and sets it `pending`. The submission semantics of section 3 (replay by key, a new key as a new revision, `already_approved`, per-item serialization) apply unchanged. It takes no photo paths and no license expiry. Changing the car after approval is not supported in this implementation (`already_approved`); it is a later card.
3. **`review_item(user_id, 'car_owner', revision, status, note)`** is the owner's manual approval, unchanged (service role only, `stale_revision`, trigger-protected statuses). The verification itself happens outside the app (for example a call); the app and the database store only the outcome and the note.
4. **`can_request_rides()`** is true for every caller whose `accounts.profile_completed_at` is set (riders request freely; section 8 and O2 are superseded). **`can_post_rides()`** is true when the caller's role is `car_owner` or `both` and the `car_owner` item is `approved` for its current revision; there is no photo requirement. Both stay `stable`, `security definer`, false for a missing row, and never raise.
5. **`my_verification()`** returns only the `car_owner` item's status, revision and reviewer note and whether car details exist.
6. **Role change (section 2C):** rider to car owner through `set_role`, then the car details screen and the pending state; `can_post_rides()` stays false until approval; car owner to rider keeps the data.
7. **Screens:** P1 to P4 (light and dark); V1b adapted as the car-owner intro without the document list; V5a without the license expiry field; V10, V11 and V12 adapted with a single car-owner row instead of document rows (V12 keeps the reviewer note and the resubmit action). **Sending states of the car submission (replaces V8 and V9 for this implementation) live on V5a itself:** while sending, the fields stay filled and disabled and the button shows progress; a failed or uncertain outcome (no response, timeout, offline) shows a retry banner, keeps every input, and retries with the **same** `submission_key`, so a lost response can never create a second submission; the screen moves to the pending view only after a server-confirmed result, either the `submit_car` result or a `my_verification()` read that shows the submitted revision. The spec's retry semantics of section 3 therefore stay fully testable without the deferred screens. Deviations from the approved frames are recorded: the expiry field and the document rows are removed and the V1b text is adapted; no Figma change is needed.
8. **Entry points (O9):** after onboarding a user with the role `car_owner` or `both` is guided to the car details screen; the Profile tab shows the car-owner status; riders see nothing.
9. **No new dependency or permission:** no `app.json` permission strings, no native rebuild, no storage configuration, no bucket.
10. **Privacy:** no identity document or number is collected or stored. The only new personal data are the profile names, consent records and the car details; plate numbers are not exposed to other users. The Egypt data-protection condition of section 9 stays in force before production data and before any later card collects documents. The `docs/product/mvp.md` amendment about government ID (O7) is no longer needed, because the product document already defers it; the vehicle field differences (colour, seat capacity) belong to the ride cards.
